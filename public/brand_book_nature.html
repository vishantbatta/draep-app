03 — The Name

Why we spell it d r a e p

The unusual spelling isn't a typo — it's the whole brand compressed into five letters. Two reasons, one vision.

draep
d r — the consonants open the door a e — the vowels sit together, included p — one consonant closes, protecting the whole
Reason 01

Everyone is included

The vowels — a and e — sit together at the very heart of the word, gathered close and never separated, while the consonants hold the space around them. It's a small act of typography that says a large thing: everyone has the right to represent themselves the way they want, and in our word — as in our world — there is room for all and everyone.

Reason 02

Challenging the status quo

"Drape" is how it has always been spelled — usual, expected, normal. We spell it differently on purpose. The moment you notice the letters are not in their usual order, you're already asking why — and that question is the point. We are here to challenge the status quo of things that simply existed because nobody questioned them.

The logo's vision The mark you see in our logo is a vision: to help everyone — each and every individual, from different sections and different factions — have the right to represent themselves. And its colors are a promise: they are the way we want to paint this world.
04 — Visual Identity

Wear Your World

A night-dark canvas so that every color — like every person — gets to be seen. The gradient is the hero; everything else makes room for it.

Color Physics

Deep violet-night backgrounds carry calm and let the bloom gradient glow. The gradient always reads left-to-right, gold into indigo — the same journey a petal takes from sunshine to shade. The gradient is the celebration, never the wallpaper.

Type Hierarchy

Poppins' rounded geometry echoes the lowercase wordmark and the friendliness of the brand; Inter keeps dense order and catalogue UI legible; IBM Plex Mono makes measurements, hex codes, and sizes look exactly as considered as they are.

Spatial Logic

Petal-soft curves everywhere — 16px cards, pill buttons, circular color dots. Glows instead of hard shadows, dashed stems instead of hard dividers. Empty space is soil: it exists so the colors can grow.

10 — The Marks

Using the Mark

Two marks, one garden: the full lockup for space to speak, the alpha bloom for small places. Both bloom best at night.

◍ lockup · on night — preferred
◍ lockup · on a petal glow
◍ lockup · on cream
◍ alpha mark · small sizes
xx xx

Clear space & minimum size

Keep a margin of x on all sides, where x = the height of the bloom mark. Nothing enters the garden but the logo. Minimum digital width: 140 px. Below that — and for favicons and app icons — switch to the alpha bloom mark alone, always on night.

✅ Do

  • Place on night backgrounds so the gradient glows
  • Use the transparent lockup — never a boxed or flattened copy
  • Use the alpha bloom mark for favicons & sizes under 140 px
  • Let it breathe — clear space is part of the design

❌ Don't

  • Rotate, skew, or re-order the wordmark — the spelling is the story
  • Re-color individual letters off-palette
  • Place directly on busy florals or mid-tone photos
  • Squash the bloom mark into a circle container

12 — Accessibility

Accessibility — WCAG 2.1 AA

A brand inspired by a flower that includes every color must include every person. Every text/background pair on this page is contrast-audited; nothing ships below AA.

Contrast, audited

Every text pair is measured, not eyeballed — see the table below. Gradient text is large-text-only where its stops clear 3:1; small labels use the 5:1+ gold-to-flamingo ink.

Keyboard & focus

A skip-to-content link, a single H1, ordered headings, and a 3:1+ focus ring on every interactive element — marigold on night, flamingo on white. Nothing is mouse-only.

Motion & visuals

All animation respects prefers-reduced-motion. Color is never the only signal — states carry icons (✓ / ✕) and text, and decorative imagery is hidden from screen readers.

Touch targets

Buttons and inputs keep a minimum 44 × 44 px hit area, comfortable for every hand.

Landmarks & alt text

Semantic header, main, sections and footer; meaningful images carry descriptive alt text; the wordmark stage is announced as "draep wordmark".

Zoom & reflow

Fluid layouts reflow to a single column at 880 px with no horizontal scrolling and no loss of content at 200% zoom.
